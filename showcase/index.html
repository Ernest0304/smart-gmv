Live pilot · Singapore · shared kitchens

Every night, someone reads 25 tablet screens and types the numbers in. That is where the billing errors come from.

Smart GMV replaces the typing. Staff photograph each delivery-platform summary screen, a vision model reads the figures, the person confirms them, and the row lands in the billing sheet with the photo attached as evidence.

It has been running nightly with real staff since late July, alongside the form it is meant to replace. The decision to switch over completely has not been taken yet — which is the honest place for a system that bills customers to be.

The round

The job is physical, and the deadline is every single night

A shared kitchen facility rents cooking space to food brands. The operator bills each brand a processing fee on the sales they made through delivery platforms between 10am and 10pm — so somebody has to know, per brand per day, what those sales actually were.

The platforms show that on a tablet in each kitchen. So at 9pm a staff member walks the corridor with a phone, reads two summary screens per kitchen, and types four numbers into a form. Twenty-five kitchens, fifty screens, a hundred numbers, every night, at the end of a shift.

A digit typed wrong at 9:40pm is an invoice wrong at month end, and nobody finds it until the customer does.

Photographing the screen instead of trusting a memory of it changes what the record is. The number stops being a claim and becomes a claim with its evidence attached.

Capture to sheet

Four steps, and the person stays in charge of the number

01

Photograph

One shot per platform. The upload starts immediately and the app moves on to the next kitchen while the read runs.

02

Read

A vision model returns orders, sales and a confidence rating — applying each platform's own arithmetic, which differs between them.

03

Confirm

Staff see the reading next to the photo and can overwrite it. Every override is stored beside the original.

04

Record

One brand-day is one row, keyed by a deterministic ID, so a retry updates the row rather than duplicating it.

Judgment calls

The decisions that made it safe to bill from

Refusal beats invention

A failed read never becomes a number

When the model is unsure or the photo is of the wrong thing, the field stays empty and the person types it. A system that guesses quietly is worse than one that stops loudly, because the guess reaches an invoice.

Idempotent writes

One brand-day is one row, however many times you press save

The record ID is derived from facility, kitchen, brand and date. A flaky connection produces a retry, not a duplicate — and a genuine correction is written as an edit with the previous value preserved.

Billing window

Round-the-clock brands get their overnight sales deducted

Some brands trade through the night, but the fee only covers 10am–10pm. Those kitchens are photographed twice, the morning reading is subtracted from the evening one, and both photos stay attached to the record.

Found in the pilot

A negative result is a contradiction, not a credit

An audit turned up a brand billed a negative amount for the day. The cause was visible in the stored photos: the morning shot was actually the previous evening's screen, so the evening reading came in below it. Now a negative figure is never written — the cell is left blank and flagged for a human.

Choosing the engine

Five models, three hundred photos, one spreadsheet of results

Picking a vision model on impressions would have been guesswork with money attached. Instead the candidates were scored against real screen photos — 300 of them in the final round — measured both against what staff had recorded that night and against where the models agreed with each other.

An earlier round had already disqualified one candidate, and not merely for being the least accurate. It was confidently wrong: it read a screen showing roughly $1,700 as a few hundred dollars, and rated itself high confidence. In a billing pipeline a wrong number that flags itself is survivable. A wrong number that looks certain is not.

3 Tiers, not one model A cheap fast reader by default, a stronger one when confidence drops, and a different vendor entirely if the first is down.

How it is put together

Boring on purpose

A phone-first web app in plain JavaScript with no build step, a single-file Python API, and Google Sheets as the store — because the finance team already lives in that sheet, and a system nobody can open and inspect is a system nobody trusts.

The interesting engineering is not in the stack. It is in the write path: session-scoped access, a column whitelist that aborts the entire write if an unrelated cell moves, evidence photos stored under content-hashed names so a retry cannot duplicate them, and an end-to-end suite that writes real rows to the real sheet and deletes them again.

Built by an operations manager who could not code three years ago, for the team he works beside.
